We are making a near-silent dry-comedy short film, 108 seconds, nine scenes of twelve
seconds each, live-action photoreal. I will send you ONE shot at a time. Each message
after this one is a complete, self-contained shot prompt. Generate exactly that shot,
nothing more, and do not carry motion, wardrobe or staging ideas from any previous shot
unless the prompt says so.

THE FILM. Our man is called SETH. He arrives at an upscale fitness club with a reasonable
plan and a text to his wife saying he'll be home in ninety minutes. The club then quietly
eats his entire evening — pickleball, cold plunge, sauna, steam room, hot tub, shower,
locker room, bar. He never notices. The audience does all the arithmetic; he does none.

SETH. Early forties. Dark brown hair, short at the sides, a little tousled on top, clearly
receding at the temples and thinning at the crown, with a high lined forehead. A short,
patchy, reddish-brown stubble beard with a few grey hairs at the chin. Black rectangular
eyeglasses. The build of a man who does not train: slender arms, narrow chest, a soft
midsection with an ordinary small belly and no abdominal definition, and a faint slouch.
He is unremarkable on purpose — the audience should recognise him as themselves, not
admire him. Never make him younger, never give him a full head of hair, never clean-shave
him, never give him muscle definition. His face arrives with each prompt as Image 1 and
that plate is the authority; this paragraph only tells you who he is so you don't drift.

THE TONAL LAW, WHICH OVERRIDES EVERYTHING. Seth is not suffering. He is enjoying his own
bewilderment. He is not a man failing at time management — he is a man with a secret
leisure life at a health club, who knows he is going to lose the evening, does it anyway,
and is completely fine with it. Correct register: warm amusement at his own theatrics,
bemused acceptance, mild private enjoyment, "here we go again." Forbidden register: grim
determination, heroic effort, gritty resolve, dread, condemnation, or satisfied
completion. If a beat could be played either grim or bemused, play it bemused. This is
the single most important instruction in this document.

SETH'S GLASSES ARE A RUNNING DEVICE. He wears them everywhere except in water and in the
shower. In heat and steam they fog — from the inside, from below, clouding upward, and
they clear from the outer edges inward. When they are fogged he keeps going anyway. When
a prompt says the lenses are clear, they are perfectly clear. Never fog them unless asked.

THE CAMERA LAW. Unless a prompt explicitly says otherwise, every shot is one continuous
locked-off static take, 12 seconds, 16:9, 35mm prime, tripod, absolute rock-solid
stillness from the first frame to the last — zero drift, zero shake, zero stabilization
float, zero pan, tilt, zoom, dolly, orbit or camera breathing. The clip must close on
precisely the framing it began with, identical down to the pixel. One motion per shot.
Multi-beat sequences are delivered to you as separate shots and cut together later; never
invent a cut, a location change or a second camera setup inside a single generation.

PHOTOREALISM. Documentary realism, not cinematic gloss. Skin is matte and organic with
visible pores, natural asymmetry and real imperfection — never plastic, never airbrushed,
never CGI sheen. Wood, tile and stone show natural grain variation, knots, imperfect
joints and colour shift — never uniform, never a repeating synthetic pattern. Fabric
drapes and creases; it never reads stiff.

TEXT IS FORBIDDEN. Never render readable text anywhere in frame — no phone screens, no
product labels, no signage copy, no depth markers, no digits. Phone screens always face
away from camera. Labels read as soft embossed shapes with no letterforms. All story text
is added later in post. A legible word in frame is a failed generation.

THE COUNT LOCKS. Every prompt ends with a short list of literal counts — how many people,
how many pairs of glasses, how many towels, how many of a specific forbidden motion.
Treat those counts as hard numeric constraints on the render, not as stylistic advice.
Zero means zero.

REFERENCE IMAGES. Each prompt attaches its own images from a local folder and refers to
them inline as @[Image 1](image_1), @[Image 2](image_2), and so on. Image 1 is always the
character. Image 2 is always the location. Any further image is a prop. "Exactly" means
exactly: match the attached face, hair, build, wardrobe and room geometry without
reinterpretation. Where a reference image contains an object the prompt says is absent —
a towel, glasses, a bottle label — the prompt wins and the object is removed.

AUDIO. Generate every shot SILENT — `generate_audio: false`. All sound is built in post.
The audio paragraph in each shot prompt describes the intended soundscape for the editor's
benefit; it is not an instruction to synthesise anything. Never add music, score, bgm,
sound design or narration.

OUTPUT. 12 seconds, 1080p, 16:9. Use Seedance 2.0 in `std` mode with `genre: comedy` and
`generate_audio: false`.

That is not an arbitrary pin. Every existing scene in this film was generated on Seedance
2.0, so switching engines mid-film risks a visible break in skin rendering and motion
character. More importantly, 2.0 exposes a `genre` parameter that Seedance 2.5 does not,
and `genre: comedy` has a verified track record here — the two Scene 7 generations that
used it both passed their register check as genuinely bemused, with no broad or slapstick
performance. The one shot generated without it came back tonally flat.

Read `genre: comedy` as a grade and pacing hint, not a licence to play broad. The comedy
in this film is behavioural and dry: it lives in stillness, in beats held a moment too
long, and in a man being reasonable about an unreasonable situation. There is never a
pratfall, never mugging, never a reaction played for the back row. If a beat could be
performed broadly or quietly, perform it quietly.

If you have a concrete reason to believe another model serves a specific shot better, say
so and wait for me rather than switching silently. After each generation, tell me the shot
ID from my prompt heading and give me the file. Do not generate the next shot until I send
it. Do not offer variations unless I ask.

REFERENCE SLOTS. Prompts refer to images as @[Image 1](image_1) and @[Image 2](image_2).
That is web-UI syntax and may not survive into an API call — if your harness does not
resolve it, map by position instead: **Image 1 is always the character plate, Image 2 is
always the location plate**, in the order the shot file lists them. The words "Image 1"
and "Image 2" inside the prompt text refer to those slots.

Confirm you have this, in one sentence, and then wait for the first shot.
